Microgravity, Root Growth and Plant Development

A literature-based research paper asking: How does microgravity alter root growth and plant development, and what does this mean for growing food during long-duration space missions?

Author: Md Nafeesur Rahman, Department of Computer Science and Engineering, East West University Date: 03 February 2026

Summary

The paper reviews published work and International Space Station (ISS) experiments, mainly on Arabidopsis and NASA's Veggie and Advanced Plant Habitat chambers. Main points:

Roots do not need gravity to set up their basic auxin pattern, but growth direction, cell walls and cell division do change.
Root length results are inconsistent across experiments, so light, water and hardware matter as much as gravity.
Leafy crops such as lettuce have been grown and eaten in space, with no major nutrient loss and no human pathogens found.
Delivering water and air to roots is the biggest practical challenge.
Small-scale fresh food is realistic for near-term missions; fully food-independent missions need better root-zone systems and more research.
